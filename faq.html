Frequently asked questions

Short answers. The methodology has the full detail.

Is Grade or Raw free?

Yes. Single-card verdicts are free and posted publicly on X, and the calculator is free to use. Some links we share are paid affiliate links, and there may be an optional tip jar. Neither changes the verdict.

How do you decide whether to grade a card?

We estimate the expected value of grading: the chance of a PSA 10 times the PSA 10 price, plus the chance of a PSA 9 times the PSA 9 price, plus the chance of 8 or lower times that price. Then we subtract the grading fee and shipping and compare the result with the raw price. We say grade only when the gain is at least $25 and at least 20% of the raw price.

Why do so many cards come out as keep raw?

Grading costs about $60 a card at PSA's cheapest open tier as of October 25, 2026, plus shipping and insurance, and most copies don't get a 10. Once you weight the PSA 10 price by its real odds and take off the costs, many cards under about $100 raw are worth more left raw.

Where do the prices come from?

Recent sold listings, not asking prices. We use the median of the last 5 to 10 sales for the raw card, PSA 9 and PSA 10, mainly from eBay sold listings and PSA Auction Prices Realized, cross-checked with TCGplayer. Fewer than 5 sales gets a low-confidence flag.

How do you estimate the chance of a PSA 10?

We start from the card's gem rate in the PSA Population Report (PSA 10 count divided by total graded), then shade it down for flaws we can see in your photos, such as off-center borders, whitening or scratches. Gem rates run high because people mostly submit their best copies.

What does grade later mean?

It means grading doesn't pay today, but the outlook score is 65 or more out of 100, based on scarcity, time out of print, Pokémon popularity, set popularity, price momentum and PSA 10 population growth. We suggest re-checking in about 90 days or when a cheaper grading tier opens.

Do you know the print run of my card?

No one outside The Pokémon Company does. It doesn't publish print runs or out-of-print dates, so we use proxies like rarity, era, population counts and sealed prices versus MSRP, and we label those scores as estimates.

Which grading company do you use?

Verdicts launch with PSA because PSA graded cards have the most sales data. The calculator lets you type any fee, so you can run the math for CGC, Beckett or another grader too.

Are the grading fees on this site current?

They were checked on October 25, 2026 and are shown with that date and a link to PSA's site. Fees change often, so the calculator treats them as editable inputs. Check psacard.com before you submit.

How do I get my card checked?

Post a clear photo of the front and back with the card name, set and number, and tag @GradeOrRaw on X. Free single checks are answered in public replies.

Is this financial advice?

No. Verdicts are opinions based on public sales data and stated assumptions. We show all the math so you can check it and decide for yourself.

Do you make money from links?

Sometimes. Some links on this site and in our posts are paid affiliate links, for example to eBay or TCGplayer. If you buy through one we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Paid links are labeled where they appear.

Are you affiliated with PSA or The Pokémon Company?

No. Grade or Raw is an independent fan project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Nintendo, The Pokémon Company, Creatures, GAME FREAK, PSA, CGC, Beckett, eBay or TCGplayer.
