Free Pokémon TCG grading advice

Honest verdicts. Most cards should stay raw.

Thinking about sending a card to PSA? Grade or Raw tells you whether grading is likely to pay off, using recent sold prices, realistic grade odds, and the full cost to grade. When the answer is "keep it raw," we say so.

How a verdict works

1. Real sold prices

We take the median of recent sold listings (not asking prices) for the raw card, PSA 9 and PSA 10. Fewer than 5 sales gets a low-confidence flag.

2. Realistic grade odds

We start from the card's PSA gem rate in the population report, then shade it down for visible flaws like centering, whitening or scratches.

3. The full cost

Grading fee plus shipping and insurance both ways. At PSA's cheapest open tier that's about $60 a card before shipping (as of October 25, 2026).

The rule we use

Expected graded value = P(PSA 10) × PSA 10 price + P(PSA 9) × PSA 9 price + P(8 or lower) × that price
Gain = expected graded value − grading fee − shipping − raw price

We say grade only when the gain is at least $25 and at least 20% of the raw price. A small positive gain is "borderline": grade it only if you want the slab for your own collection. Then we add a forward outlook (grade now, grade later, or keep raw) based on scarcity, how long the card has been out of print, and how popular the Pokémon and set are. Read the full methodology.

Why "keep raw" comes up so often

A PSA 10 price looks great on its own. But most copies don't get a 10, the fee and shipping come off the top, and you wait months. For a lot of cards under about $100 raw, the expected value of grading comes out below the raw price. An honest service should tell you that.
